# Chart Standards

Charts answer a decision question and use editable PowerPoint chart objects whenever practical.

- Horizontal bar: rank concentration or compare categories; sort by value and label directly.
- Column: compare discrete periods or options; always include units.
- Line: show time or maturity progression; avoid more than four series.
- Waterfall: explain additive change only when the components are declared and comparable.
- Heat map: label both axes and every status; combine color with text or pattern.
- Maturity progression: use named stages, not false decimal precision.
- Impact-tolerance consumption: show elapsed time and the declared tolerance in minutes or hours.
- Concentration comparison: label counts as relationship counts, never risk or probability.
- Investment prioritization: use qualitative bands or bounded ranges with assumptions stated.

Use Continuum Navy for the baseline, Signal Teal for the recommended or validated state, Copper for decisions, Slate Blue for evidence, and semantic colors only for defined status. Minimize gridlines, prohibit three-dimensional effects, and place the source and interpretation in speaker notes.
